## 1. Logline

> *In a living world made of candy and consequence, one broken soldier must cross a shattered continent to answer a question no one else will ask: what actually happened to the world, and whether it can be put back together.*

## 2. World Description — The Sweetlands

**Status: VERIFIED (world concept) / PROPOSED (specific regions and geography)**

The Sweetlands is a world composed entirely of living confectionery matter — a vast, breathing geography of crystalline sugar plains, gelatin forests, hard-candy mountain ranges, syrup rivers, and licorice root-warrens beneath the earth. Everything is alive in some sense: stone is slow-crystallizing rock candy, soil is compressed sugar sediment, water is flavored syrup in various viscosities. The flora and fauna are made of the same fundamental substance as the people.

At the physical and metaphysical foundation of this world lies the **Sugar Lattice**: an invisible crystalline framework of interlocking sugar-crystal bonds stretching through the entire geography, providing structural stability to landmasses, cohesion to large bodies of water, and the substrate through which magical forces (Gel Reserve, Flavor energies, Conviction) propagate. It is the bedrock of the world — not a sky god, not a metaphor, but a material fact of physics in this reality.

The people of the Sweetlands — the twelve **Lineages** — are gummy creatures of diverse compositions, flavors, and forms. They build civilizations on sugar-crystal foundations, craft weapons from tempered licorice and crystallized bone-candy, and wage wars across stretches of taffy marshland and hard-candy cliffs. They are fully conscious, fully mortal, and fully capable of tragedy.

### The Shattered Sweetlands — Post-Event Geography

**Status: PROPOSED — requires narrative owner approval**

The inciting event of the game has already occurred before the story begins. The **Shattering** — a catastrophic fracturing of the Sugar Lattice — tore the continent apart. Large fragments of land now float at various altitudes above a churning syrup sea. Some float stably; others drift; others collide slowly and catastrophically. The world's geography is now vertical as much as horizontal.

### Physical Laws

The following are VERIFIED properties of the world relevant to gameplay design:

- **Gel is the fundamental substrate.** All living creatures in the Sweetlands are composed of some form of candy gel. This is why GelFlow physics apply universally — stretch, compression, crystallization, melting, and adhesion are not exotic phenomena but basic material properties of life.
- **Flavor is real force.** Cherry Ember, Blue Frost, Lime Sour, and similar flavor affinities are not just cultural identity — they represent genuine thermochemical and energetic properties. A Cherry Ember Bearkin runs hotter. A Blue Frost Jellybean Folk is structurally more brittle under impact but more resistant to heat.
- **Sugar Wells are natural restoration points.** Where the Sugar Lattice is intact or only partially fractured, upwelling crystalline sugar energy creates Sugar Wells — physical locations that restore body integrity. These are not magical conveniences but geological features.
- **The Shattering changed everything.** Pre-Shattering culture, architecture, and ecology must be contrasted with post-Shattering survival culture throughout all writing.

## 3. Central Conflict — The Shattering of the Sugar Lattice

**Status: PROPOSED — central antagonist concept UNKNOWN, requires owner decision**

### What Happened

The Sugar Lattice fractured simultaneously at hundreds of nodes across the continent. No natural event — no earthquake, no volcanic chain, no magical accident — could explain a synchronized multi-node fracture. The fracture pattern was deliberate. Someone or something caused the Shattering intentionally.

This is the central mystery of the main questline. The player begins after the Shattering has already happened, in a world that has been trying to survive and adapt for an indeterminate period (PROPOSED: approximately two years post-Shattering). Everyone knows the Shattering happened. Almost no one knows why — or if they do, they are not saying.

### Central Antagonist

**Status: UNKNOWN — owner decision required**

The identity, motivation, and nature of the entity or force responsible for the Shattering is an open narrative decision. Design space includes:
- A faction that believed the Lattice was a prison rather than a foundation
- A monarch or noble who miscalculated the consequences of a ritual
- An external force predating the Sweetlands entirely
- A distributed error — no single villain, but a cascading system failure caused by collective negligence

**No antagonist concept should be locked in without narrative owner approval.**

### Immediate Consequences

1. **Mass displacement.** Millions of gummy people were separated from their home fragments, their families, their infrastructure.
2. **Resource scarcity.** The Sugar Lattice provided not just stability but the slow circulation of nutrients through the world-body. With it fractured, food production is disrupted.
3. **Political collapse and reformation.** The old monarchies that depended on Lattice-stable territories now rule floating rubble. New factions have formed to fill the vacuum.
4. **Restoration question.** Can the Sugar Lattice be repaired? By whom? At what cost? This is the central gameplay/narrative tension.

## 4. Tone Guide

### The Four Tonal Pillars

GUMFALL operates in four tonal registers simultaneously. These are not separate modes — they coexist in single scenes, sometimes in single lines of dialogue.

#### 4.1 Epic Fantasy

The world is genuinely large, genuinely stakes-high, and genuinely capable of grandeur. The Sweetlands before the Shattering was a real civilization with history, philosophy, art, architecture, and interregional politics spanning centuries. The player's journey is consequential. The antagonist's plan (whatever it is) has world-scale implications. Royal Courts have genuine power. The Sugar Lattice is not a cute detail — it is the foundation of the world.

**Writing check:** Does this moment feel like it matters? Would a character who has seen a thousand years of Sweetlands history weep at this?

#### 4.2 Tragedy

The Shattering was a real catastrophe. Lineages lost their ancestral homes. The Staleborn are already a dying people; the Shattering may be their extinction event. The Licoricefolk's underground networks were torn open. The Jawbreaker Kin's mountain fortresses now orbit in space. Real suffering must not be undercut by whimsy unless the character doing the undercutting is using humor as a defense mechanism — and that distinction must be legible in the writing.

**Writing check:** Has this character paid a real price? Does the world register loss?

#### 4.3 Physical Comedy

Bodies stretch. Bodies bounce. Bodies lose limbs and then look for them in embarrassing places. A Bearkin Gummy Brawler who takes a direct blow from a Crusher may fly thirty feet through the air, land face-first in a taffy bog, and pull themselves out one arm at a time. This is funny. It is also real. It can be both simultaneously.

Physical comedy in GUMFALL is never mean-spirited toward the player. The humor is about the physical absurdity of the world's material reality, not mockery of failure.

**Writing check:** Is the body horror funny or just horrifying? Does the joke land on the world, not on the player's competence?

#### 4.4 Sincere Heroism

The player character is trying to do something genuinely hard for genuinely good reasons. Companions believe in something. NPCs who sacrifice themselves mean it. The game does not ironically undercut heroism. Characters may be cynical, jaded, or broken — but the narrative arc is toward meaning, not away from it.

**Writing check:** Would a player feel embarrassed being sincerely moved by this? If yes, something is wrong.

## 5. Voice Guidelines

### World Voice

- The Sweetlands has its own idiom. Characters do not say "hell" or "damn" — they say "melt," "crystallize," "shatter," or invoke flavor deities.
- Common expressions: "What in the Lattice—", "Sugar and shards!", "By the Pour—", "That's a hard-candy problem."
- The world has its own profanity, but it is candy-native. Avoid Earth imports unless thematically deliberate.

### Character Voice

Each lineage has a cultural voice. This is a tendency, not a rule — individual characters deviate.

| Lineage | Voice Tendency |
|---------|----------------|
| Bearkin | Direct, loyal, occasionally blunt to the point of social awkwardness |
| Wormkin | Oblique, patient, comfortable with long pauses and metaphor |
| Ringborn | Formal, hierarchical, conscious of rank and tradition |
| Gumdrop Folk | Warm, communal, storytelling-oriented |
| Jellybean Folk | Quick, mercurial, emotionally transparent |
| Bottleborn | Merchant-class pragmatism, deal-framing |
| Sourlings | Dry humor, deliberate understatement, dark comfort with difficulty |
| Licoricefolk | Layered, complex, trust earned slowly |
| Marshfolk | Literal, environmentally grounded, discomfort with abstraction |
| Jawbreaker Kin | Terse, endurance-oriented, respect through silence |
| Drakegums | Proud, territorial, archaic grammar structures |
| Swarmkin | Collective voice patterns, consensus phrasing, difficulty with singular perspective |

### Player Character Voice

The player character is **silent protagonist by default** — reactions communicated through animation, companion commentary, and player choices rather than voiced lines. (PROPOSED: voice options may be made available as accessibility features in character creation.) All companion dialogue should be written assuming the PC's response is a player choice from a menu.

### Tonal Guardrails for Writers

1. **Never mock the player's lineage or composition.** Jokes about body type or appearance come from antagonists or established comic foils, never from the game's voice.
2. **Physical comedy is universal.** All lineages experience physical comedy equally. The game does not single out any lineage as the butt of physical jokes.
3. **Tragedy earns its weight.** Do not introduce a character for the purpose of killing them for pathos in the same scene. Let loss accumulate.
4. **Heroism is available to everyone.** No background, lineage, or class is narratively positioned as less capable of genuine heroism.

## 6. Thematic Pillars

### Pillar 1: Integrity Under Pressure

The game's central physical mechanic — body integrity, the tracked health of each body region — is also its central theme. What holds together under pressure? What fractures? Can what has been broken be restored, or does it become something else?

This applies at every scale: a body region losing structural integrity during combat, a character losing their sense of self after displacement, a political faction losing coherence under military pressure, a world losing its physical foundation.

**Not:** resilience as a feel-good concept.
**Yes:** integrity as a material property that can be scientifically examined, measured, and sometimes restored — and sometimes cannot.

### Pillar 2: The Cost of Connection

Gummy bodies can physically connect — reattach limbs, fuse, bind. The Adhesion stat tracks how readily a character bonds with surfaces and people. Factions form and fracture. Companions trust and lose trust. The world is literally held together by bonds.

What does it cost to form bonds? What is lost when they are severed? The Sugar Lattice is a metaphor for this theme — but only in retrospect. It must first be real, physical, consequential.

### Pillar 3: Memory and Change

The Staleborn are literally living repositories of old memory — candy that has grown stale and crystallized, preserving itself against change. They represent a faction that chose preservation over adaptation. The Memory Keepers (PROPOSED faction) are their institutional expression.

Every character in GUMFALL carries what they were before the Shattering and must decide what to become after it. Legacy Reroll (Rebatching) is not just a mechanical system — it is an expression of this theme. You can start over. You carry something forward. The question is what.

### Pillar 4: The Right to Reconstruction

Who gets to decide how the world is rebuilt? The Crown Accord wants to restore the old hierarchies. The Free Pour wants a new order. The Gelwright Circle wants a scientifically managed reconstruction. The Void Wrappers want the fracture to become permanent. The Memory Keepers want the old world archived before it is rebuilt.

The player navigates these competing claims. GUMFALL does not have a canonically correct political answer — the player's choices shape which reconstruction happens, and all outcomes have costs.

- No faction is purely evil or purely good.
- The Crown Accord includes genuine protectors of the vulnerable and also landed nobles protecting their own privilege.
- The Free Pour includes genuine egalitarians and also opportunists.
- The Void Wrappers include sincere believers in Wrapperborn destiny and also nihilists using religious language as cover.
- Player characters can work with all factions, oppose all factions, or navigate between them.

## 8. Political Landscape Overview

The political structure of the Sweetlands post-Shattering is organized into three overlapping layers:

### Layer 1: The Floating Fragment States

Each major floating fragment is effectively a city-state. Fragments with surviving monarchies have attempted to reconstitute their governance. Fragments that lost their leadership in the Shattering have improvised governance — councils, military commanders, or factional control.

### Layer 2: The Royal Courts

The seven Royal Courts (see [World & Dungeons](../world/WORLD_AND_DUNGEONS.md)) represent pre-Shattering political entities that have survived in some form. They include:
- The Cindercoil Warrens (Kobold monarchy)
- The Red Banner Labyrinth (Hobgoblin dominance hierarchy)
- The Moonfang Court (Gnoll tribal confederation)
- The Root-Crown Hollows (Troll territorial claim)
- The Pale Sugar Crypt (Staleborn memory kingdom)
- The Ashglass Wyrm Throne (Dragon hegemony)
- The Folded Court (Wrapperborn dimensional sovereignty)

**IMPORTANT:** Do not expose exact Royal Court quest solutions, maze layouts, or sovereign specific rewards in public documentation.

### Layer 3: The Transient Networks

Trade routes, smuggling networks, refugee camps, and itinerant factions that do not claim fixed territory. Bottleborn merchant fleets, Wormkin underground networks, and Swarmkin distributed communities operate at this layer.

## 9. Endgame Story Direction

**Status: PROPOSED — requires narrative owner approval before development**

The endgame narrative asks the player to make a choice about the future of the Sugar Lattice. The three PROPOSED paths:

1. **Full Restoration:** The Sugar Lattice is rebuilt, the fragments reconnect, the old geography is partially reconstituted. High political cost — the Crown Accord wins significant legitimacy.
2. **Adaptive Reconstruction:** The Lattice is partially restored in a new pattern, creating a different but stable geography. The Gelwright Circle's approach. Lower political cost, higher technical risk.
3. **Permanent Fracture:** The Shattering is accepted as the new reality. New infrastructure (Wrapperborn fold-bridges, Bottleborn fleet networks) replaces the Lattice. The Free Pour and Void Wrappers see this as their vindication.

Each path has gameplay implications for post-game content, world state, and companion outcomes.

**No ending is the "good ending."** Each has irreversible costs.

## 10. Key Narrative Questions

**Status: UNKNOWN — open design questions requiring answers before main questline development**

1. Who or what caused the Shattering, and with what intent?
2. Is the Sugar Lattice a natural feature of the world or something that was created — and if created, by whom?
3. What is the player character's pre-game history? Are they amnesiac, do they have an established backstory, or is this determined by Background selection?
4. What is the relationship between Flavor affinities and the Lattice? (Do flavors represent fragments of Lattice nodes?)
5. Is there life outside the Sweetlands? (Is the world the only gummy world, or are there others?)
6. What exactly are the Void Wrappers' beliefs about what the Shattering revealed?
7. Can the Staleborn be "restored" or are they permanently on the path to full crystallization and death?
8. What is the Folded Court's actual territorial claim — are they claiming part of the Sweetlands, or a separate folded-space reality?
9. What happened to full cities during the Shattering? Mass casualties, evacuation, or something stranger?
10. Does the player character have a canonical lineage in the opening cinematic, or is the red Bearkin soldier the tutorial companion, not the player character?

## 11. Opening Tone Reference — The First Line

The first words a player hears in GUMFALL are (VERIFIED — see [Intro Cinematic](INTRO_CINEMATIC.md)):

> *"Before we go anywhere, I would like everyone to count their arms."*

This line sets the entire tonal contract:
- **Epic scope** (it follows a cinematic showing the entire world, armies, catastrophe)
- **Physical comedy** (counting arms — because losing them is real and commonplace)
- **Sincere heroism** (the soldier saying it is missing an arm — it's not a throwaway joke, it's lived experience)
- **Dark absurdity** (the most grounded possible response to a world-ending event)
- **Warmth** (the soldier addresses "everyone" — the player is already being included)

Every piece of writing in GUMFALL should be able to trace a line back to the spirit of that sentence.

## 12. Accessibility Considerations for Narrative

- All narrative content must support subtitle display with speaker identification.
- Cutscene text must be re-readable via in-game journal or codex.
- No critical narrative information should be conveyed through audio alone.
- Tone variations (comedy/tragedy balance) should be documented in accessibility notes so players with mood-sensitivity preferences can understand what to expect from each region.
- Content warnings should be available in the main menu for themes including: body horror (PG, candy-context), loss, war, and displacement.
