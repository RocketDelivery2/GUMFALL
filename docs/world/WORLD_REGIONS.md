## World Overview

**[PROPOSED]** The Shattered Sweetlands is a world of living confectionery matter — a realm where terrain, weather, and flesh are all composed of structured sugar, gelatin, candy compounds, and crystalline mineral-sweets. The world is not metaphorically candy-themed; it is literally built from these materials, and its inhabitants — gummy creatures, kobolds, hobgoblins, gnolls, trolls, dragons, Staleborn, Wrapperborn, and player characters — are all made of these substances in varying compositions.

**[PROPOSED]** Before the catastrophe, the Shattered Sweetlands formed a single continuous landmass called the Great Pan — a vast, stable expanse of layered sugar-stone, gelatin aquifers, caramel bedrock, and crystallized sky-sugar reefs. The Great Pan was held together at its deepest level by an interlocking network of crystalline energy conduits known collectively as the **Sugar Lattice**: an ancient, semi-sentient infrastructure that distributed structural cohesion, warmth, ambient light, and life-sustaining energy across the entire world.

**[PROPOSED]** The Sugar Lattice catastrophic shattering — called simply **the Shattering** by most inhabitants — broke this infrastructure apart. The consequences were immediate and ongoing: the Great Pan fractured into thousands of floating geographic fragments of wildly varying size, altitude, and stability. Some fragments rose thousands of feet above where the old ground once lay. Others plummeted into the **Underpan** — the deep sub-stratum of molten sugar-rock and ancient heat that underlies everything. Fragments continue to drift, collide, and occasionally fuse or break apart, making the Shattered Sweetlands a genuinely dynamic world.

**[UNKNOWN]** The cause of the Shattering is a central narrative mystery. Owner decision required on the true origin. The following factions have conflicting explanations:
- **Crown Accord [PROPOSED]:** The Shattering was caused by anti-royal insurgents tampering with Lattice anchor points.
- **Free Pour [PROPOSED]:** The old monarchy weaponized the Lattice as a control mechanism and lost control.
- **Gelwright Circle [PROPOSED]:** The Lattice was a natural system that was overtaxed by population expansion and industrial extraction.
- **Memory Keepers [PROPOSED]:** The Staleborn remember the actual moment, but their accounts contradict each other.
- **Void Wrappers [PROPOSED]:** The Shattering was intentional purification and they intend to finish what began.

**[DERIVED]** Because server authority governs movement, contact, and body states (see technical design), all physics interactions with the Shattered Sweetlands terrain — floating platforms, falling fragments, surface adhesion — must be server-resolved to maintain fairness.

## The Sugar Lattice Event

**[PROPOSED]** The Sugar Lattice was not merely infrastructure — it was a living network of crystallized energy that predated all recorded civilization in the Sweetlands. Scholars of the Gelwright Circle [PROPOSED] theorize it was grown, not built, emerging over geological spans from mineral-sweet deposits reacting to deep Underpan heat.

**[PROPOSED]** When the Lattice shattered, the following world-scale effects occurred and persist to the present day:
- Gravity became locally variable. Fragments drift at different altitudes and some rotate slowly.
- Sugar Lattice remnant shards still carry residual energy and are both a resource and a hazard.
- **Crown Depth** — a vertical stratification of the world — emerged as the Lattice's energy gradient became physically apparent as altitude layers. (See [Secret Areas and Royal Dungeons](./SECRET_AREAS_AND_ROYAL_DUNGEONS.md) for Crown Depth mechanics.)
- Living creatures who died at the moment of Shattering were crystallized into the **Staleborn** — a new form of preserved undead life.
- **Wrapperborn** — creatures that had always existed in dimensional folds between spaces — were suddenly exposed as those folds tore open.
- The **Void** — a conceptual non-space referenced in Wrapperborn theology [PROPOSED] — became partially accessible through unstable Fold zones.

**[PROPOSED]** The Shattering is not a historical event that has passed and been forgotten. It is an ongoing crisis. New fractures occur. Fragments fall. The Lattice remnants still destabilize. The world is actively hostile to the idea of stability, which is the fundamental tension of life in the Sweetlands.

## Fractured Flats

### Description
**[PROPOSED]** The Fractured Flats are the entry zone of the Shattered Sweetlands — a sprawling network of low-altitude fragments ranging from small platforms barely large enough for a campfire to broad plains-fragments the size of old counties. The terrain is uneven and pragmatic: chunks of caramel-stone jut from sugar-soil, shallow candy-grass plains interrupt rocky outcroppings, and the edges of most fragments offer sheer drops into the inter-fragment void.

**[PROPOSED]** The Flats were once farmland and open countryside. Evidence of old Pre-Shattering structures — half-collapsed sugar-brick walls, abandoned mill equipment, sunken aquifer shafts — appears regularly. Most surface settlements are built on the larger fragments, and the population of the Flats is the most mixed in the world: kobold warrens occupy the smallest rocky shards, roaming gnoll bands cross fragment-bridges, and small human-equivalent gummy communities cluster around anchor-points where the old Lattice ran near the surface.

### Atmosphere
**[PROPOSED]** Rustic, weathered, familiar-but-wrong. The sky is wide and unobstructed here — players can often see dozens of other fragments drifting at various distances. The light is warm and diffuse, filtered through crystallized atmospheric particulate. Wind carries faint sugar-mineral scent. The Flats feel like a starting place — because they are — but the scars of the Shattering are impossible to ignore.

### Dominant Monster Families
- **Kobolds [VERIFIED]** — surface warrens, tunnel networks, trap-layer behavior
- **Gnolls [VERIFIED]** — roaming pack hunters crossing fragment terrain
- **Surface Gummy Creatures [PROPOSED]** — ambient wildlife native to low-altitude fragments
- **Lattice Scavengers [PROPOSED]** — creatures that feed on residual Sugar Lattice energy deposits

### Level Range
**[PROPOSED]** 1–25. Designed as the primary introductory and early-game region.

### Notable Landmarks
**[PROPOSED]**
- **The First Crack** — A canyon-scale fracture in one of the largest Flats fragments where the Shattering originated according to local oral tradition. A pilgrimage site and beginner dungeon entrance.
- **Cindercoil Warrens** — The kobold royal court compound, tunneled into a deep sub-fragment network. (See [Royal Courts Public Catalog](./ROYAL_COURTS_PUBLIC_CATALOG.md).)
- **Fragment Bridge Row** — A series of permanent rope-and-crystal bridges constructed between six large fragments, forming the main trade route of the Flats.
- **The Anchor Spine** — A visible ridge of dead Sugar Lattice conduit running along the surface of one large fragment. No longer functional. Researchers from the Gelwright Circle [PROPOSED] maintain a presence here.
- **Saltpan Market** — A fragment repurposed as a neutral trade hub. No faction officially controls it, though this is disputed.

### Faction Presence
**[PROPOSED]**
- **Crown Accord:** Maintains patrol posts on larger fragments. Claims governance authority.
- **Free Pour:** Active cell presence in fragment settlement communities. Disputes Crown Accord taxation.
- **Gelwright Circle:** Researchers at Anchor Spine, small workshop nodes.
- Kobold clans nominally acknowledge the Cindercoil Warrens crown structure but village-level autonomy is high.

### Special Terrain Rules
**[PROPOSED]**
- **Fragment Edge Fall:** Falling off fragment edges drops players into the inter-fragment void. Respawn at nearest discovered anchor point.
- **Lattice Deposit Zones:** Glowing ground patches where residual Lattice energy surfaces. Provides minor regeneration buff. Can also attract Lattice Scavengers.
- **Low Adhesion Zones:** Some fragment surfaces are candy-glass-smooth. Adhesion-dependent movement abilities have reduced effectiveness.
- **Bridge Integrity:** Fragment bridges can be damaged during combat. Some are player-destructible.

### Material Resources
**[PROPOSED]**
- Sugar-stone (construction grade)
- Caramel ore (base crafting metal)
- Candy-grass fiber (light armor component)
- Lattice shard fragments (low-grade enchanting material)
- Kobold-forged tin (trade good, drops from Cindercoil enemies)

## Crystalline Highlands

### Description
**[PROPOSED]** The Crystalline Highlands occupy the mid-altitude band of the Shattered Sweetlands — a dramatic landscape of towering crystal formations, mineral-sweet geysers, and broad flat-top mesas rising from a maze of lower ridges. The fragments here are older and denser than the Flats, composed primarily of compressed crystal-sugar and mineral-sweet compounds that formed deep in the old Great Pan's geological layers before the Shattering thrust them upward.

**[PROPOSED]** Hobgoblin civilization dominates the Highlands — a militarized, hierarchy-driven culture that occupies fortified positions on defensible mesa-tops and ridge lines. Hobgoblin banner-posts mark territorial boundaries and their patrols follow rigid routes. Their architecture is angular, permanent, and clearly designed by an organized military mind.

### Atmosphere
**[PROPOSED]** Austere and sharp. The crystalline formations catch and scatter light in prismatic bursts, making the Highlands visually spectacular but also disorienting. Sound carries oddly — voices echo strangely through crystal formations, and combat audio has a distinctive bright, ringing quality. The air is colder than the Flats and carries a mineral-sugar bite.

### Dominant Monster Families
- **Hobgoblins [VERIFIED]** — organized formations, chain-of-command behavior
- **Crystal Elementals [PROPOSED]** — animated sugar-crystal constructs
- **Highland Predators [PROPOSED]** — large gummy apex creatures adapted to crystal terrain

### Level Range
**[PROPOSED]** 20–45.

### Notable Landmarks
**[PROPOSED]**
- **Red Banner Labyrinth** — The hobgoblin royal court structure. (See [Royal Courts Public Catalog](./ROYAL_COURTS_PUBLIC_CATALOG.md).)
- **The Prismatic Spine** — A natural formation of enormous crystal columns that serves as a navigational landmark visible from most of the Highlands and some of the Flats below.
- **The Broken Parade Ground** — Pre-Shattering military infrastructure, now partially floating. Hobgoblin units use it for drills. A significant loot opportunity and a dangerous encounter zone.
- **Gelwright Survey Station Delta** — A Gelwright Circle research outpost studying crystal-sugar geology.
- **The Banner Sea** — An open mid-altitude area between Highlands fragments where hobgoblin banners on floating poles mark claimed aerial territory.

### Faction Presence
**[PROPOSED]**
- **Crown Accord:** Formal alliance with the Hobgoblin royal structure. Crown Accord emissaries maintain a permanent delegation at Red Banner Labyrinth outer courts.
- **Free Pour:** Operates through smuggling routes between Highlands fragments. Assists non-hobgoblin populations in Crown Accord-controlled areas.
- **Gelwright Circle:** Survey and research presence. Politically neutral.

### Special Terrain Rules
**[PROPOSED]**
- **Crystal Resonance:** Certain attack types (Shock, Spirit) cause crystal formations to vibrate and shatter, creating environmental hazards from fragments.
- **Reflective Surfaces:** Some abilities that involve projectiles or energy beams interact with crystal surfaces in unexpected ways. Intended as a skill-expression mechanic.
- **Formation Advantage:** Hobgoblin encounters are designed around formation behavior. Players who break formations gain advantage; players who fight into formations face multiplicative difficulty.
- **Altitude Exposure:** Without proper equipment, extended time in the upper Highland band applies a cold-exposure status.

### Material Resources
**[PROPOSED]**
- Crystal-sugar (high-grade enchanting base)
- Mineral-sweet ore (mid-tier crafting)
- Prismatic shard (optical component for Candy Artificer blueprints)
- Hobgoblin forged iron (trade good, drops from Red Banner enemies)
- Geyser-salt (alchemical component)

## Sourswamp Delta

### Description
**[PROPOSED]** The Sourswamp Delta is a collection of low-lying, fog-heavy fragments that drift in loose clusters at altitudes just above the Fractured Flats' lower range. The terrain is waterlogged — the swamp fragments retain large quantities of citric acid fluid in shallow pools, dense reed-tangles of sour-grass, and spongy boggy ground that moves underfoot. Visibility is consistently poor due to a permanent acid-fog that forms when Underpan heat vents interact with the citric pools.

**[PROPOSED]** Gnolls dominate the Delta in a fundamentally different way than hobgoblins dominate the Highlands: where hobgoblins hold fixed positions, gnolls move constantly, their pack structures organized around hunting routes and seasonal prey migration patterns. Gnoll culture in the Delta is territorial in a fluid, scent-marked way — boundaries exist but shift. The Moonfang Court [VERIFIED] maintains the ritual center of gnoll Delta culture.

### Atmosphere
**[PROPOSED]** Oppressive and sensory-overwhelming. The acid fog limits visibility to short range. Sound is muffled. The color palette is sour greens, yellows, and muddy browns. Glowing bioluminescent plants provide the primary ambient light at ground level. Movement is sticky and slow unless players invest in Sourswamp-appropriate equipment or stat bonuses.

### Dominant Monster Families
- **Gnolls [VERIFIED]** — pack hunters, scent-based tracking
- **Acid Flora [PROPOSED]** — animate sour-plants that attack or create hazards
- **Bog Lurkers [PROPOSED]** — ambush creatures that hide in the citric pools
- **Delta Parasites [PROPOSED]** — small creatures that swarm in enormous numbers

### Level Range
**[PROPOSED]** 15–40.

### Notable Landmarks
**[PROPOSED]**
- **Moonfang Court** — Gnoll royal court. (See [Royal Courts Public Catalog](./ROYAL_COURTS_PUBLIC_CATALOG.md).)
- **The Fog Gate** — A natural canyon between two large Delta fragments where the acid-fog is densest. Gnolls use it as an ambush choke point. Players who master it gain efficient traversal routes.
- **The Moonpool** — A large open pool of bioluminescent fluid used for gnoll ritual. One of the few locations in the Delta with clear visibility due to the light driving back the fog.
- **Driftwood City** — An improvised settlement built on a collection of lashed-together small fragments. Non-gnoll population center. Perpetually semi-submerged in fog and perpetually under negotiated gnoll tolerance.

### Faction Presence
**[PROPOSED]**
- **Crown Accord:** Minimal direct presence. Uses gnoll tribal agreements for access rather than direct governance.
- **Free Pour:** Strong sympathizer network in Driftwood City and other non-gnoll Delta settlements.
- **Memory Keepers:** Maintain a small observation post near ancient Staleborn ruins partially submerged in a Delta fragment.

### Special Terrain Rules
**[PROPOSED]**
- **Acid Fog Visibility:** Ranged attack range is reduced in fog-heavy zones. Fog density is dynamic and affected by wind mechanics.
- **Bog Movement:** Movement speed reduced in deep bog terrain. Agility-focused characters have mitigation options.
- **Sour Exposure:** Prolonged contact with citric pool fluid applies Sour damage-over-time. Fortitude derived stats affect resistance.
- **Scent Tracking:** Gnolls that detect players can call packs. Breaking line-of-sight does not break gnoll pursuit — scent must be masked or distance must exceed pack-call range.
- **Bioluminescence Interaction:** Certain spells and abilities interact with the bioluminescent flora — some trigger it to flash (blinding nearby enemies), others cause it to dim (increasing concealment).

### Material Resources
**[PROPOSED]**
- Citric-sap (Sour damage component)
- Sour-grass fiber (resistance crafting material)
- Bog-crystal (low-grade, but Sour-aspected enchanting material)
- Gnoll-bone (trade good, rare drop)
- Bioluminescent oil (lighting component and alchemical ingredient)

## Sugar Coast

### Description
**[PROPOSED]** The Sugar Coast is the name given to the collection of edge-facing fragments — those fragments whose outer face presents to the open void rather than to other fragment clusters. The Coast fragments form the literal margins of the known Sweetlands. Beyond their outer edges, the inter-fragment void extends to the horizon, and in clear conditions, distant uncharted fragment clusters are visible as shapes on the horizon.

**[PROPOSED]** Coastal fragments experience stronger wind, more dramatic weather, and direct exposure to what locals call **the Void Breath** — the energy emanating from the open inter-fragment space that has no name in academic taxonomy. The Sugar Coast supports a diverse mixed population: maritime-adapted gummy communities, independent raider bands, merchant convoys, and significant Wrapperborn presence near the Fold-adjacent coastal margins.

### Atmosphere
**[PROPOSED]** Dramatic and open. The wind is constant. The color palette shifts from warm sugary tones to cooler blues and grays at the void-facing margins. Weather events — candy-crystal storms, sugar-rain squalls — arrive from the open void and sweep inward. The Coast feels expansive and dangerous in equal measure.

### Dominant Monster Families
- **Coastal Raiders [PROPOSED]** — mixed-composition pirate/raider bands
- **Void-Touch Creatures [PROPOSED]** — animals affected by prolonged void exposure
- **Maritime Gummies [PROPOSED]** — large sea-adapted gummy creatures navigating the inter-fragment void
- **Wrapperborn Scouts [PROPOSED]** — advance presence near coastal Fold seams

### Level Range
**[PROPOSED]** 10–35.

### Notable Landmarks
**[PROPOSED]**
- **The Sugarbreak Docks** — A large merchant fragment used as a waypoint for inter-fragment trade. Nominally neutral, functionally chaotic.
- **The Void Wall** — The outer edge of the largest Coast fragment, where the drop into the open inter-fragment void is sheer and thousands of feet. A dramatic visual landmark and a location with narrative significance [UNKNOWN].
- **The Wrapmist Shore** — A stretch of Coast fragments adjacent to Wrapper Fold Zone seams where Fold-energy produces visible shimmer effects and occasional dimensional distortions.
- **Cape Saltcrystal** — A promontory fragment rich in salt-crystal deposits and home to an independent community that trades with all factions and swears allegiance to none.

### Faction Presence
**[PROPOSED]**
- **Crown Accord:** Patrol ships (fragment-sailing vessels) maintain a presence on the primary trade routes.
- **Free Pour:** Strong Coast presence. The independent maritime communities are the ideological heart of Free Pour anti-royalist sentiment.
- **Void Wrappers:** Infiltrating coastal communities near Fold seams.
- **Gelwright Circle:** Monitoring Void Breath energy readings and Fold activity.

### Special Terrain Rules
**[PROPOSED]**
- **Wind Force:** Strong wind at Coast margins affects projectile trajectory and light-body characters' movement.
- **Void Exposure:** Extended time at the void-facing extreme edge without protection applies a Void-exposure status [PROPOSED] — details to be determined.
- **Weather Events:** Candy-crystal storms reduce visibility, apply Cold damage, and can damage unprotected equipment over sustained exposure.
- **Maritime Fragment Travel:** Some Coast content involves boarding and traversing fragment-sailing vessels — mobile combat arenas.

### Material Resources
**[PROPOSED]**
- Salt-crystal (preservation and alchemical use)
- Void-touched mineral (rare enchanting component)
- Coastal candy-timber (construction and weapon hafts)
- Maritime organism drops (varied)
- Storm-crystal (high-energy enchanting material, rare)

## Molten Underpan

### Description
**[PROPOSED]** The Molten Underpan is not a floating fragment region — it is the deep sub-surface layer of the Shattered Sweetlands, the geological basement that persists beneath the drifting fragments. When the Great Pan shattered, the Underpan did not break away; it remained as a continuous surface of ancient molten sugar-rock and geothermal heat. Fragments that plummeted during the Shattering now float at various heights above it, and some have embedded partially into the Underpan surface.

**[PROPOSED]** Dragons dominate the Underpan. Their biology — heat-resistant, capable of sustained deep-heat breath, structurally adapted for burrowing through caramel-bedrock — makes them the apex predators of the sub-surface world. The Ashglass Wyrm Throne [VERIFIED] is built into the Underpan's deep layer.

**[PROPOSED]** The Underpan is accessed through vertical shafts in Flats-level fragments, through natural cracks that opened during the Shattering, and through constructed descent structures in settlements that have economic reasons to reach the sub-surface layer (primarily mining and geothermal energy extraction).

### Atmosphere
**[PROPOSED]** Ancient, oppressive, and magnificent. The Underpan has no sky — the underside of floating fragments forms a chaotic ceiling at varying heights. Heat vents create column effects. The light is orange and red — geothermal glow and heat-radiation from the molten sugar-rock. Sound is deep and resonant. Dragons are rarely seen but always felt as a presence.

### Dominant Monster Families
- **Dragons [VERIFIED]** — apex predators, territorial, heat-aspected
- **Caramel-Borers [PROPOSED]** — burrowing creatures that tunnel through bedrock
- **Underpan Flora [PROPOSED]** — heat-adapted life forms growing from geothermal vents
- **Deep Gummies [PROPOSED]** — ancient, heat-adapted gummy creature varieties

### Level Range
**[PROPOSED]** 35–65.

### Notable Landmarks
**[PROPOSED]**
- **Ashglass Wyrm Throne** — Dragon royal court. (See [Royal Courts Public Catalog](./ROYAL_COURTS_PUBLIC_CATALOG.md).)
- **The Great Vent** — A geothermal megavent that rises from the Underpan surface high enough to create a visible column of heat-shimmer visible from the Flats above. A navigational landmark and a significant thermal hazard zone.
- **The Embedded City of Slagmaw** — A pre-Shattering city that partially survived by being at the right depth when the Shattering occurred. Now half-buried in solidified molten sugar-rock, accessible but dangerous, and occupied by a mixed community of Underpan survivors.
- **The Root Network** — A system of tunnels bored by Caramel-Borers over generations, forming an interconnected navigation system through the Underpan bedrock. The passages are accessible to players but sized for ambush.

### Faction Presence
**[PROPOSED]**
- **Crown Accord:** Maintains Underpan mining operations under treaty with the Ashglass dragon clans.
- **Gelwright Circle:** Geothermal research and deep-material extraction.
- **Free Pour:** Supports independent Slagmaw City communities resisting Crown Accord extraction contracts.

### Special Terrain Rules
**[PROPOSED]**
- **Heat Exposure:** Underpan surface areas apply ambient Heat damage to players without appropriate heat resistance. The resistance requirement scales with proximity to major geothermal features.
- **Molten Floor Hazard:** Some areas have accessible molten sugar-rock surfaces. Contact is lethal without specialized equipment or very high Fortitude.
- **Ceiling Hazard:** The underside of embedded fragments creates overhead crush and impact hazards during certain dragon encounters.
- **Burrowing:** Some enemies can burrow through Underpan bedrock and emerge beneath players.
- **No Direct Fragment Travel:** The Underpan is not fragment-hopping territory. Vertical transit systems (lifts, shafts, dragon-bored tunnels) are the primary travel mechanisms.

### Material Resources
**[PROPOSED]**
- Ashglass (dragon-shed material, high-tier crafting)
- Geothermal crystal (Heat-aspected enchanting material)
- Caramel-bedrock ore (deep construction material)
- Dragon scale fragments (premium crafting drops)
- Deep-candy mineral (rare alchemical component)

## Wrapper Fold Zones

### Description
**[PROPOSED]** The Wrapper Fold Zones are not a geographic location in the conventional sense — they are regions of the Shattered Sweetlands where the dimensional membrane that separates the physical world from the **Void** [PROPOSED — a conceptual non-space in Wrapperborn theology and culture] is thin, damaged, or absent. These zones appear as visible distortions in the air, spatial anomalies where distances behave wrongly, and locations where creatures from the Fold — Wrapperborn and Fold entities — can cross freely.

**[PROPOSED]** Before the Shattering, Fold Zones were rare and small, known to scholars but not threatening to everyday life. The Shattering tore the dimensional membrane in hundreds of locations simultaneously, and the Fold Zones have been expanding since. Some overlap with other regions — there are Fold Zone intrusions in the Sugar Coast, the Crystalline Highlands, even near the Fractured Flats — but the primary Fold Zone clusters form their own distinct exploration region.

**[PROPOSED]** Wrapperborn society [VERIFIED] is built around the Fold. The Folded Court [VERIFIED], ruled by the Monarch Facing Inward and She Who Stands Behind [VERIFIED], occupies a space that exists simultaneously inside the Fold and in the physical world.

### Atmosphere
**[PROPOSED]** Deeply wrong in a way that is difficult to describe. Visual space is unreliable — objects appear at incorrect distances. Colors are desaturated or inverted near deep Fold seams. Sound arrives out of temporal order in extreme cases. Movement through Fold-space feels like moving through resistance that has no physical source. Wrapperborn who inhabit these zones appear perfectly comfortable; players and other non-Wrapperborn creatures typically do not.

### Dominant Monster Families
- **Wrapperborn [VERIFIED]** — dimensional-fold inhabitants, inside-out biology
- **Fold Entities [PROPOSED]** — non-material creatures native to Void-adjacent space
- **Fold-Touched Creatures [PROPOSED]** — ordinary creatures warped by sustained Fold exposure

### Level Range
**[PROPOSED]** 40–70.

### Notable Landmarks
**[PROPOSED]**
- **The Folded Court** — Wrapperborn royal court. (See [Royal Courts Public Catalog](./ROYAL_COURTS_PUBLIC_CATALOG.md).)
- **The Torn Seam** — The largest single Fold Zone tear known, where the void is visible as a tangible surface. Standing in front of the Torn Seam is described as looking into a mirror that shows a world where everything is on the inside.
- **The Void Wrappers' Sanctum [PROPOSED]** — A faction base operating within a Fold Zone, making conventional siege impossible.
- **The Threshold Archive** — A Wrapperborn cultural site containing records that exist both inside and outside the Fold simultaneously. The Memory Keepers maintain a diplomatic presence here.

### Faction Presence
**[PROPOSED]**
- **Void Wrappers:** Home faction. Actively expanding Fold Zone presence.
- **Wrapperborn (non-Void-aligned):** The Folded Court maintains political neutrality while managing Fold stability.
- **Gelwright Circle:** Highly active research presence studying Fold mechanics. Tense relationship with Void Wrappers.
- **Memory Keepers:** Diplomatic and archival presence.

### Special Terrain Rules
**[PROPOSED]**
- **Spatial Distortion:** Navigation in deep Fold Zones uses Fold-orientation mechanics rather than standard compass/map. Player minimap distorts in Fold-heavy areas.
- **Fold Phasing:** Wrapperborn and Fold entities can phase through conventional terrain features inside Fold Zones. Players who acquire Fold-affinity items have limited phasing access.
- **Inside-Out Hazard:** Deep Fold Zones invert some physical properties — a mechanic to be determined that creates genuine disorientation without being anti-accessibility.
- **Void Resonance:** Spirit-type abilities behave differently in Fold Zones — some are amplified, some are suppressed. Specifics to be determined per ability.

### Material Resources
**[PROPOSED]**
- Fold-membrane shard (unique enchanting material for void-aspected abilities)
- Wrapper-silk (rare material from Wrapperborn craft tradition)
- Void crystal (high-tier Spirit-enchanting component)
- Dimensional residue (alchemical reagent)
- Torn-seam fragment (narrative/lore item with crafting potential)

## Staleborn Cryptlands

### Description
**[PROPOSED]** The Staleborn Cryptlands are fragments that were, at the moment of the Shattering, populated communities, civic centers, or sacred sites. The Shattering crystallized everyone present who died in that instant — and many did — into the Staleborn: preserved, crystallized, conscious, and grief-struck remnants of the pre-Shattering world. The fragments themselves are similarly affected: buildings, roads, markets, and infrastructure are preserved in a crystallized state, frozen in the moment before catastrophe.

**[PROPOSED]** The Staleborn [VERIFIED] are not mindless undead. They remember. They remember what the world was, who they were, who they loved, and what they lost. The Cryptlands are communities of the crystalline preserved, living out a half-existence between the world that was and the world that is. The Pale Sugar Crypt [VERIFIED], ruled by King Aldren and Queen Miraline [VERIFIED], is the political center of Staleborn civilization.

**[PROPOSED]** The Memory Keepers faction [PROPOSED] originated in the Staleborn communities and serves as cultural preservation advocates and historical record holders.

### Atmosphere
**[PROPOSED]** Melancholy, beautiful, and deeply strange. The Cryptlands are visually magnificent — crystallized pre-Shattering architecture preserved in perfect detail, lit from within by the residual energy in the Staleborn themselves. The atmosphere is quiet. The Staleborn do not speak loudly; the Cryptlands have the quality of a library or a memorial. Violence here feels like a desecration — which is used deliberately to inform player tone guidance.

### Dominant Monster Families
- **Staleborn (hostile) [PROPOSED]** — crystallized remnants who have broken from grief into aggression
- **Cryptland Parasites [PROPOSED]** — creatures that feed on crystallized energy from Staleborn or preserved structures
- **Memory Horrors [PROPOSED]** — entities formed from collective traumatic memory crystallization

### Level Range
**[PROPOSED]** 30–60.

### Notable Landmarks
**[PROPOSED]**
- **Pale Sugar Crypt** — Staleborn royal court. (See [Royal Courts Public Catalog](./ROYAL_COURTS_PUBLIC_CATALOG.md).)
- **The Preserved Market** — A pre-Shattering market district frozen in the moment of the Shattering. Stalls are still stocked. Prices are still posted. Customers are crystallized mid-transaction.
- **The Hall of Before** — The Memory Keepers' primary archive. Contains crystallized memory records — objects that, when touched, transmit pre-Shattering experiential memory.
- **The Grief Gardens** — A series of fragment-gardens maintained by Staleborn who channel their loss into cultivation. Some of the rarest preserved botanical materials are found here.
- **The Cracked Ones' Hollow** — A Cryptland area dominated by Staleborn who have fractured from grief — neither fully themselves nor simple enemies. A moral and mechanical challenge zone.

### Faction Presence
**[PROPOSED]**
- **Memory Keepers:** Home faction. Primary political voice for Staleborn interests.
- **Crown Accord:** Formal relationship with the Pale Sugar Crypt monarchy — the oldest of the Crown Accord's royal relationships.
- **Free Pour:** Complicated relationship. Supports Staleborn autonomy in principle; many Staleborn find modern political categories meaningless given their perspective.

### Special Terrain Rules
**[PROPOSED]**
- **Crystalline Fragility:** Some preserved structures are extremely fragile. Area-effect attacks may cause structural collapse that changes encounter geometry.
- **Memory Resonance:** Touching certain preserved objects triggers memory fragments — short narrative sequences. These can provide lore and, in some cases, mechanical hints for secrets.
- **Grief-Field:** In areas of densest Staleborn occupation, a passive emotional resonance field applies a focus-disruption effect — a subtle mechanical expression of the Cryptlands' emotional weight.
- **Crystallization Risk [PROPOSED]:** Extremely high Spirit damage in Cryptland areas carries a crystallization status risk that needs design review before implementation.

### Material Resources
**[PROPOSED]**
- Preservation crystal (unique — stabilizes other materials, prevents decay)
- Staleborn-memory shard (lore item, also Intellect-enchanting component)
- Pre-Shattering artifact (narrative item category — historical objects with various uses)
- Grief-crystal (Spirit-aspected enchanting material)
- Crystallized botanical (rare ingredient for high-tier recipes)

## Crown Tier

### Description
**[PROPOSED]** The Crown Tier is the name for the collection of fragments that occupy the highest stable altitude band in the Shattered Sweetlands. These fragments rose furthest during the Shattering and have remained at elevation — held there, according to Gelwright research, by concentrated Sugar Lattice energy remnants that were once part of the Lattice's uppermost distribution network.

**[PROPOSED]** The Crown Tier is home to the most powerful political entities in the post-Shattering world. All seven Royal Courts [VERIFIED] have at least a formal presence or tributary relationship in the Crown Tier, though their actual dungeon structures are distributed across the lower regions. The Crown Tier fragments are themselves the seat of power for several of the royal families' diplomatic and administrative functions.

**[PROPOSED]** Access to the Crown Tier requires traversal of the Crown Depth vertical system — the stratification of altitude that gates progression. Only players who have navigated the intermediate regions reach the Crown Tier as a natural progression endpoint.

### Atmosphere
**[PROPOSED]** Elevated in every sense. The Crown Tier has breathtaking views — the entire Shattered Sweetlands is visible below in scattered fragment clusters. The light here is the cleanest in the world, unfiltered by lower atmospheric layers. Architecture on Crown Tier fragments is the most elaborate: formal, imposing, built for permanence by cultures that intend to hold power. The air carries a faint crystallized sweetness that is the highest concentration of Sugar Lattice remnant energy anywhere accessible.

### Dominant Monster Families
- **Elite variants of all seven royal monster families [DERIVED from royal court structure]**
- **Crown Guard [PROPOSED]** — cross-faction elite units serving Crown Tier administrative functions
- **Lattice Remnant Entities [PROPOSED]** — powerful creatures formed from concentrated Lattice energy at this altitude

### Level Range
**[PROPOSED]** 55–80+. Royal Dungeon content within the Crown Tier may scale higher for endgame content.

### Notable Landmarks
**[PROPOSED]**
- **The Accord Summit** — Crown Accord's primary administrative center. The closest thing the post-Shattering world has to a capital.
- **The Conclave of Crowns** — A neutral meeting space on one Crown Tier fragment where representatives of all seven royal courts can convene. Theoretically. In practice, attendance is contested.
- **The Lattice Spire** — The highest known surviving section of intact Sugar Lattice conduit. Gelwright Circle has established an observatory and research center. The Spire is also a key location for endgame narrative content [UNKNOWN].
- **The Forbidden Approach** — The outer boundary of the Crown Depth's Forbidden layer [VERIFIED concept]. Players who approach this boundary encounter escalating resistance without conventional combat explanation. (See [Secret Areas and Royal Dungeons](./SECRET_AREAS_AND_ROYAL_DUNGEONS.md) for Crown Depth details.)

### Faction Presence
**[PROPOSED]**
- **Crown Accord:** De facto administrative control. This is their home territory.
- **Free Pour:** Covert presence only. Actively hunted by Crown Accord in this zone.
- **Gelwright Circle:** Research access granted by treaty.
- All seven royal families: diplomatic representation.

### Special Terrain Rules
**[PROPOSED]**
- **Altitude Pressure:** Without appropriate gear or stat investment, Crown Tier altitude applies a periodic stamina drain.
- **Lattice Resonance:** High density of Lattice remnant energy means all Spirit-type abilities are amplified. Enchantments on equipped items have a mild passive bonus in Crown Tier areas.
- **Sovereign Territory:** PvP rules in Crown Tier areas are governed by area-specific consent rules — certain Crown Tier zones have lawful/unlawful designations based on faction control.
- **Crown Guard Enforcement:** Crown Guard NPCs actively intervene in combat near administrative structures — zone-specific law enforcement mechanic.

### Material Resources
**[PROPOSED]**
- Crown crystal (highest-tier generic enchanting material)
- Lattice-pure shard (endgame enchanting component)
- Sovereign-grade alloy (endgame crafting material)
- Royal tribute goods (trade goods obtained through Crown Tier faction systems)
- Forbidden-layer material [PROTECTED — drop rates and exact nature not disclosed publicly]

## Inter-Region Travel

### Overview
**[PROPOSED]** Travel between the floating fragments of the Shattered Sweetlands is a core gameplay and world-building mechanic. Multiple systems exist with different risk/reward profiles.

### Primary Travel Methods

**Fragment Bridges [PROPOSED]**
Permanent physical bridges constructed between closely adjacent fragments. The most common form of inter-region travel within a fragment cluster. Variable construction quality — some are ancient pre-Shattering infrastructure, some are player-or-NPC-built post-Shattering. Some bridges are faction-controlled with tollpoints. Some are destructible in conflicts.

**Fragment Sailing [PROPOSED]**
Vessels that traverse the inter-fragment void using Lattice-energy sails and wind. Used for longer distances, especially between fragment clusters and to the Sugar Coast. Vessels vary from small personal skiffs to large merchant ships to military frigates. Combat at sea (inter-void) is a designed experience.

**Crystal Lattice Transit [PROPOSED]**
Remnant Sugar Lattice conduit networks, where still-functional sections, provide rapid transport between connected nodes. Travel is near-instantaneous but nodes are fixed, often in contested or dangerous areas, and the conduits themselves can be disrupted. Gelwright Circle maintains many of the functional nodes.

**Leap Points [PROPOSED]**
Gravity-anomaly locations where Shattering-era physics continue to operate — fragments in close proximity with a void between them that players can traverse with sufficient momentum. Some Leap Points are reliable; others shift. Requires appropriate movement ability investment or momentum management.

**Warpgate Equivalents [PROPOSED — needs design validation]**
Whether the game includes fast-travel systems of a more conventional game design nature (menus, teleportation) is an open design question. Any such system must be consistent with the world's physical logic and not trivialize traversal challenge.

**[ASSUMPTION]** All inter-region travel should involve meaningful choice and risk rather than being frictionless fast-travel. Travel is a designed experience, not administrative overhead.

## Regions and Progression Eras

**[PROPOSED]** The eight regions are not meant to be visited in strict linear sequence. The design intent is structured exploration freedom: players have a clear early-game starting point (Fractured Flats) and a clear late-game destination range (Crown Tier), but the middle regions allow lateral progression and encourage players to find routes that fit their class, playstyle, and curiosity.

| Era | Regions | Character Focus |
|---|---|---|
| Early (1–25) | Fractured Flats primary; Sugar Coast introductory access | Establishing core combat, learning GelFlow, first secret areas |
| Mid (20–45) | Crystalline Highlands, Sourswamp Delta, Sugar Coast full | Class specialization deepening, first Royal Dungeons |
| Deep Mid (35–65) | Molten Underpan, Staleborn Cryptlands, Wrapper Fold Zones | Advanced encounters, multi-region Royal Dungeons, Mythic Sanctum access |
| Late (55–80+) | Crown Tier primary; all regions for endgame content | Full system mastery, all Royal Courts, Unbound Secret access |

**[DERIVED]** Ranked PvP divisions (Copper through Unbound) do not map directly to region level ranges but correlate loosely — see [PvP Foundations](../multiplayer/PVP_FOUNDATIONS.md).

**[ASSUMPTION]** The level cap is 100 for base content with Unbound levels above this requiring additional design decisions. See attribute and progression documentation.
